About plot

A letter from the founder.

For years my watchlist was a note on my phone, my reviews were texts to friends, and release dates were whatever I happened to remember. It mostly worked, which is the worst kind of broken. So I built Plot. The note became a watchlist that knows when and where everything is streaming. The texts became reviews, character-capped by design. The calendar makes up for my patchy memory, always knowing when the next episode lands.

Why I built plot

You don't have to call yourself a cinephile to belong here. If you've ever rewatched the same show three times because nothing else felt right, you're the person I made this for.

Plot started because of a real lack of enthusiasm for the apps that already exist. They're clunky, seemingly only ever in dark mode, and bluntly, no fun to look at. A place to track what you watch shouldn't be hard on the eyes or a chore to open.

Then there was the pressure. The pressure of writing reviews, of performing how you felt about something in the "right" way. Plot is for people who love TV, who love movies, who love the cinema and aren't afraid to say exactly how something made them feel. If all you have to say about a movie is that it made you cry 14 separate times, firstly, shout out to my emotional baddies, and secondly, thanks for the heads up! That one's getting saved for when a good cry is needed.

Sometimes the only review worth writing is "I fucking loved that", and then moving on.

Reviews that take ten seconds

That's why Plot caps the characters in your reviews. The point is to capture your immediate, true reaction, the way you would in a journal. Nobody delivers a measured critique the second the credits roll. You have a feeling. That's what movies and TV are designed to give us. They're not made to be picked apart frame by frame.

Rate it, say how it hit you, move on. Months later you'll scroll back through your Plot and remember exactly who you were when you watched it. That's the whole product: the joy of watching, minus the performance of it.

What plot does

Building in this space, you notice the small frustrations you'd usually ignore. Never knowing when a show drops or the next episode lands. Forgetting the movie a friend swore by. The nightly hunt across seven platforms, only to give up and pick the first thing on Netflix. Plot remembers all of it, so you don't have to.

Better with your people

Plot is quieter than a social network and better than a group chat. Follow a friend and you'll see what they're watching and what they thought, in their own words, not a 2,000-word essay. Compare your taste and settle the argument about whose recommendations are better. Share your calendar with family so they know not to call you during the Summer House reunion.

The fastest way to get someone on Plot is to send them your taste match. Nobody can resist finding out they're a 41.

What's next

I've got a lot more I want to do. Native apps for iPhone and Android. Push and email alerts the moment something on your watchlist arrives. Smarter, more personal recommendations. And watch together, so you and your friends can line up the same show and watch in real time, wherever you are. Everything that has already shipped is on the changelog, newest first.

Built by one person

Plot is built by one person, around a full-time job. There's no team, no investors and no corporate roadmap. It's shaped almost entirely by feedback from the people who use it, which means you. Any note, good or bad, any feature request or bug you spot, lands in my inbox and usually ends up in the app. You can send one anytime from Settings, or email contact@theplot.tv.

Plot is still early, so if you're using it now, you're an early user too, bugs and all. When something breaks or feels off, tell me. I don't take that kind of patience lightly, and it's already more than enough.

If you ever want to do more than that, going Premium is the biggest help. Plot is also on Ko-fi for anyone who'd rather just buy the builder a coffee. Both cover what it actually costs to run, and both buy me more time to keep building.

Thank you for reading this. Thank you for considering Plot. And if you're already a user, thank you for backing a solo builder trying to make a life out of building beautiful things.

Questions people ask

Is Plot free?

Yes. Tracking, your watchlist, the release calendar, What's On, where to watch, up to 5 lists and following friends are all free, forever, with no credit card. Premium adds unlimited lists, a live calendar subscription and Pick for Me for US$3/month or US$24/year, with a 7-day free trial.

How is Plot different from Letterboxd, Trakt or IMDb?

Plot covers movies and TV together, in one journal, in daylight. Reviews are short on purpose, so you log a feeling instead of writing an essay. And Plot leans into what happens next: a release calendar, where to watch on every title, and a daily What's On page.

Is there a Plot app for iPhone or Android?

Not yet. Plot runs in any browser at app.theplot.tv and works well on a phone. Native iPhone and Android apps are in progress, and you can ask to be notified on the home page.

Can I import my history from another app?

Yes. Import your watch history from Letterboxd, Trakt, Netflix or TMDB exports in Settings. You confirm every entry before it's saved, your existing Plot ratings and notes stay untouched, and duplicates are skipped.

Who owns my data?

You do. New profiles start private, so only followers you approve see your activity, and private notes are only ever visible to you. Plot doesn't sell your data and runs no ads. You can export everything, and the full detail is in the privacy policy.
