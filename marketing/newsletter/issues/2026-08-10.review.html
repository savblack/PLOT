What everyone's talking about
One Hundred Years of Solitude has reopened the adaptation question: how much of a novel lives in the telling rather than the events? The Independent admires the series' visual reach but finds its boundary between ordinary life and magic less fluid than the book's. Moneycontrol responds more warmly to the care in Macondo's decline. Both reactions make the same invitation more specific. Come for a richly imagined family saga; decide for yourself whether seeing this world makes it more tangible or less mysterious.
One Night Only has prompted a conversation about what happens when a sex comedy starts negotiating its own limits. In a film-podcast community discussion, viewers debate the reported test-screening response and the idea of designing a film around audience feedback. That thread is a debate, not proof of what caused the finished film's problems. But the question is worth carrying into the movie: if your premise promises a night unlike ordinary life, how cautious can the comedy afford to be?
